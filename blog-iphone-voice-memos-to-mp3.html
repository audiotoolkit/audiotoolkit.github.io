How to Convert iPhone Voice Memos to MP3

iPhone's Voice Memos app saves recordings as M4A files. That's fine for most things, but if you need to upload one somewhere that only accepts MP3, or share it with someone whose software doesn't like M4A, here's the quickest path — no app installs, no emailing files to yourself.

Step 1: Get the file out of Voice Memos

  1. Open the Voice Memos app and tap the recording you want.
  2. Tap the share icon (the square with an arrow pointing up).
  3. Choose Save to Files, and pick a location like "On My iPhone" or iCloud Drive.

You now have an M4A file sitting in the Files app, ready to upload anywhere.

Step 2: Convert it to MP3

  1. On your iPhone (or any device), open the M4A to MP3 converter.
  2. Tap the upload area and choose the file you just saved from the Files app.
  3. Tap Convert & Download. The MP3 saves straight back to your device.

The whole thing happens in your browser — the recording is never uploaded to a server, which matters if the memo is personal.

Why not just email it to yourself?

Emailing a file to yourself doesn't convert the format — you'd still have an M4A attachment, just in your inbox instead of Files. It also means the recording passes through a mail server, which is an unnecessary extra step if you're specifically trying to avoid that for something private.

Does converting reduce the quality?

Slightly — converting between two lossy formats (M4A's AAC and MP3) causes a small amount of additional quality loss from re-encoding. For a spoken voice memo, this is essentially never audible. If you want zero re-encoding loss and don't need broader compatibility, there's no need to convert at all.
