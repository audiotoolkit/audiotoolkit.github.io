How It Works

How to Increase Audio Volume

1. Upload your audio. Drag and drop a file into the upload area, or click to browse your device.

2. Choose Manual Boost or Normalize. Manual Boost lets you set an exact percentage. Normalize automatically finds the loudest safe level for you.

3. Watch for the clipping warning. If your chosen boost would distort the audio, a warning appears — back off the slider or switch to Normalize.

4. Apply and download. Your louder file saves straight to your device as a WAV file.

Why It Matters

Boosting Without Distortion

Every digital audio file has a hard ceiling — push the volume past it and the waveform "clips," creating a harsh, crackling distortion that can't be undone afterward. Most volume boosters online just multiply blindly and let you find out the hard way.

This tool checks your file's actual peak level and warns you in real time if your chosen boost would exceed that ceiling, before you download anything.

Good To Know

Manual Boost vs. Normalize

Use Manual Boost when you know roughly how much louder you want the file — useful for matching the volume of one clip to another.

Use Normalize when you just want the loudest possible volume without any distortion, and don't want to guess a percentage. It automatically calculates the exact safe ceiling based on your file's current peak.

Neither fixes background noise. Boosting raises everything in the recording equally, including any hiss, hum, or background noise — it won't clean up a noisy recording, only make it louder.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between boosting and normalizing? ▾
Boosting multiplies the volume by a fixed amount you choose, which can distort if pushed too far. Normalizing automatically raises the volume to the loudest safe level without any distortion, based on the quietest and loudest points already in your file.
Will boosting the volume cause distortion or clipping?
It can, if you boost too aggressively. This tool shows a live warning when your chosen boost level would cause clipping, so you can back off before exporting a distorted file.
Can normalizing fix a very quiet recording?
Normalizing raises the overall volume up to the loudest point the file can handle without clipping, which helps a lot with quiet recordings. It can't remove background noise or fix a recording that's quiet due to being far from the microphone in a way that also affects clarity.
Is my audio file uploaded to a server?
No. Volume changes are calculated and applied locally in your browser using the Web Audio API. Your file is never uploaded.
