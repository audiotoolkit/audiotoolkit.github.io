Why It Matters

What Sample Rate Actually Means

Sample rate is how many times per second an audio signal is measured to represent it digitally — 44,100 times per second for standard CD-quality audio, for example. A higher sample rate can represent higher frequencies faithfully; a lower one can't capture as much high-frequency detail, but produces a smaller file.

This tool resamples using the Web Audio API's own high-quality resampling, run through an offline rendering context, so it happens quickly and precisely without any audio being uploaded.

Good To Know

Choosing a Sample Rate

44.1 kHz is the universal standard for music, matching CD audio — use this unless you have a specific reason not to.

48 kHz is the standard used in video and film production — convert to this before adding audio to a video editing timeline.

22.05 kHz or 16 kHz are sometimes required by older voice/telephony systems, IVR platforms, or speech recognition tools, and produce meaningfully smaller files for voice content where very high frequencies aren't important.
