How It Works

How to Convert MP3 to OGG

1. Upload your MP3. Drag and drop it into the upload area, or click to browse your device.

2. OGG is tried first. If your browser supports it, OGG is pre-selected; if not, this tool switches to WAV automatically and tells you why.

3. Convert and download. OGG encoding runs in real time, so a progress bar shows how far along it is — it takes roughly as long as the audio itself.

Why It Matters

Why Does This One Work Differently?

Browsers include a fast, instant encoder for MP3 and WAV, but not for OGG. To produce real OGG (Opus) audio, this tool has to actually play the file through your browser's built-in recorder in real time and capture the result — there's no shortcut. It's still entirely local; nothing is uploaded, it just isn't instant.

If your browser doesn't support OGG recording at all (some browsers on some devices don't), this tool checks for that upfront and switches you to WAV rather than producing a broken or silently mislabeled file.

Good To Know

Good to Know

Best browser support: recent Chrome, Edge, and Firefox on desktop generally support OGG/Opus recording. Some mobile browsers and Safari may not — this tool will tell you and fall back to WAV automatically.

Keep the tab active. Since encoding happens in real time, avoid switching away from the tab or letting your device sleep mid-conversion.
