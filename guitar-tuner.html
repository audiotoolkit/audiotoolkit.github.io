How It Works

How to Tune Your Guitar

1. Click "Start Tuner" and allow microphone access when prompted.

2. Pluck one string and let it ring clearly, away from background noise.

3. Watch the needle. It centers and turns green when you're in tune; drift left means flat (tune up), drift right means sharp (tune down).

4. Repeat for each string. The six chips show standard guitar tuning (E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4) and highlight the closest match.

Why It Matters

How Pitch Detection Works

This tuner uses an autocorrelation algorithm — it compares the incoming audio waveform against slightly delayed copies of itself to find the repeating pattern that reveals the fundamental frequency. This is a standard, well-established technique for detecting the pitch of a single sustained note, and it runs entirely in your browser without any audio being recorded or sent anywhere.

Good To Know

Getting an Accurate Reading

Mute other strings. Sympathetic ringing from nearby strings can confuse the pitch detector — lightly rest your other fingers on unused strings if needed.

Tune in a quiet space. Background noise, music, or conversation can interfere with an accurate reading.

Works for any instrument. This is a chromatic tuner under the hood — the string labels are just a shortcut for standard guitar tuning, but any note you play will be detected and named correctly.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this only work for guitar? ▾
No. This is a chromatic tuner, meaning it detects any pitch and shows the nearest note name, so it works for any instrument — bass, ukulele, violin, or even voice. The string labels shown are just a convenience for standard guitar tuning.
Is my audio recorded or sent anywhere?
No. The microphone audio is analyzed in real time directly in your browser to detect pitch, and is never recorded, stored, or sent anywhere.
Why isn't it detecting my note accurately?
Background noise, a very quiet pluck, or multiple strings ringing at once can confuse pitch detection. For best results, tune in a quiet room and pluck one string cleanly, letting it ring for a moment before reading the display.
What tuning does this use?
Standard concert pitch, where A4 = 440 Hz. This covers standard guitar tuning (E A D G B E) and works for any other instrument tuned to standard pitch.
