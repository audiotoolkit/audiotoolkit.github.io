How It Works

How to Use Text to Speech

1. Type or paste your text. Up to 5,000 characters at a time.

2. Pick a voice, speed, and pitch. Available voices depend on your device and browser.

3. Click Speak. The text is read aloud immediately through your speakers.

Why It Matters

Using Your Browser's Own Voices

This tool uses the Web Speech API, a standard feature built into modern browsers, to access the text-to-speech voices already installed on your device — the same ones used by your phone's accessibility features or screen reader. That means your text is spoken locally on your device and is never sent to a server to be processed.

Good To Know

Voice Availability Varies

Available voices depend on your device. iPhones, Android phones, and Macs generally ship with more natural-sounding neural voices than some desktop browsers on Windows or Linux, where voice options can be more limited or more robotic-sounding.

This tool can't produce a downloadable audio file — browsers don't expose their built-in speech synthesis as a recordable stream. If you need an audio file, you'd need separate recording software to capture what plays.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do the voices come from? ▾
This tool uses your device and browser's own built-in text-to-speech voices, via the standard Web Speech API. The available voices and their quality vary by device and browser — more are usually available on a phone or Mac than some browsers on Windows.
Is my text sent to a server?
No. The text is spoken using your device's own speech engine directly in the browser. It is not uploaded or sent anywhere.
Can I download the speech as an audio file?
Not currently — browsers don't provide a way to capture their built-in speech synthesis output directly as a file. This tool plays the speech live; you would need separate screen/audio recording software to capture it as a file.
Why do some voices sound more natural than others?
Voice quality depends entirely on what's built into your operating system and browser, not on this tool. Mobile devices and Mac computers often include higher-quality neural voices than some desktop browsers.
