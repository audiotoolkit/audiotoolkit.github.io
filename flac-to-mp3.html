About This Conversion

About FLAC and MP3

FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec) compresses audio without discarding any data, so it sounds identical to the original source — popular with audiophiles, high-resolution music stores, and music production. The trade-off is file size: FLAC files are much larger than compressed formats.

MP3 remains the most universally compatible audio format, supported by essentially every device and platform ever made. Converting FLAC to MP3 trades some of that lossless detail for dramatically smaller files and much broader compatibility.

Good To Know

Choosing the Right Bitrate

For general listening: 256–320 kbps keeps the conversion very close to the FLAC source for most music.

If storage space matters more than audiophile-level accuracy: 192 kbps is still a solid, widely-used middle ground.

A note on the MP3 encoder: conversion relies on an encoder library loaded from a public CDN. If it fails to load, this tool automatically switches to WAV export instead of silently failing.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Why convert FLAC to MP3? ▾
FLAC preserves full lossless audio quality but produces much larger files — often 5–10 times the size of an equivalent MP3. Many phones, car stereos, and older media players don't support FLAC at all, while MP3 plays almost everywhere.
Does converting FLAC to MP3 reduce audio quality?
Yes, some. FLAC is lossless, so converting to the lossy MP3 format discards some detail. At 256–320 kbps, this is very difficult to hear for most listeners on typical playback equipment.
Is my FLAC file uploaded to a server?
No. Your file is decoded and converted locally in your browser using the Web Audio API. It is never uploaded anywhere.
Can my browser actually open FLAC files?
Most modern browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari) can decode FLAC natively. If decoding fails, try updating your browser or use a different one.
