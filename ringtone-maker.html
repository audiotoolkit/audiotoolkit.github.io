How It Works

How to Make a Ringtone

1. Upload your song or audio clip. Drag and drop it into the upload area, or click to browse your device.

2. Pick your favorite part. Drag the handles under the waveform to select the hook, chorus, or moment you want as your ringtone — ideally 15 to 30 seconds.

3. Choose MP3 or WAV. MP3 works directly as an Android ringtone; either works as a starting point for iPhone via GarageBand.

4. Preview and download. Listen to your selection, then download the finished clip.

Why It Matters

Being Upfront About iPhone Ringtones

Apple's ringtones use a specific .m4r file format, which is really just an AAC-encoded M4A file renamed with a different extension and a length restriction. Producing a properly-encoded AAC file reliably in every browser isn't something we can guarantee works consistently, so rather than offer a ".m4r download" that might not actually function as a real ringtone, this tool is honest about exporting MP3 or WAV instead.

The good news: getting a clip onto an iPhone as a ringtone from there is a well-documented, free process using GarageBand (available on every iPhone) or Finder on a Mac.

Good To Know

Tips for a Great Ringtone

Pick a recognizable moment. The chorus hook or most memorable few seconds of a song usually makes a better ringtone than the intro.

Keep it short. 15–20 seconds is usually enough to recognize a call; anything much longer just delays you answering.

Add a fade if it cuts off abruptly. If your selection starts or ends mid-note, run it through the Fade In/Out Adder afterward for a cleaner sound.
