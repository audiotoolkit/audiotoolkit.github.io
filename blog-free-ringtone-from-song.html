5 Free Ways to Make a Ringtone from Any Song

Turning a song into a ringtone is really just trimming it down to a short clip and getting it onto your phone in the right format. Here are five ways to do that, compared honestly on what's easy and what has caveats.

1. A browser-based audio trimmer

Upload the song, drag to select the 15–30 second section you want, and download the clip. Works on any device, doesn't require installing anything, and if the tool processes locally, your file never leaves your device. The main limitation: setting an MP3 or M4A file as an actual ringtone (not just a saved audio clip) still requires a device-specific step afterward, covered below.

2. iPhone: GarageBand

Apple's free GarageBand app can trim a song and export it directly as a ringtone through Settings, without needing iTunes or a computer. It's a legitimate option already on many iPhones, though it's more app than most people need for a simple 20-second trim.

3. Android: a file manager and manual placement

On Android, you can trim a clip (with any trimmer tool), then move the resulting file into your device's Ringtones folder using a file manager app, after which it appears as an option in Sound settings. Slightly more manual than iPhone's route, but doesn't require any extra app if you already have the trimmed clip.

4. Desktop audio editing software

Free desktop tools like Audacity can trim and export a clip, which you then transfer to your phone via cable or cloud storage. More setup than a browser tool, but useful if you want finer editing control — fades, precise cuts, or combining multiple sections — before exporting.

5. Phone's built-in voice memo / clip tools

Some phones include basic trimming directly in their default media apps. It's the most limited option in terms of precision and export flexibility, but for a quick, rough cut with nothing extra installed, it's an option worth knowing about.

Which one to actually pick

For most people, a browser-based trimmer is the fastest path: no installs, works identically on any device, and gets you a clean clip in under a minute. The remaining step — setting that clip as your actual ringtone — depends on your phone's OS, and is a short, well-documented process either way.
