How It Works

How to Trim an Audio File

1. Upload your audio. Drag and drop a file into the upload area, or click to browse your device. The waveform appears as soon as it's decoded.

2. Set your start and end points. Drag the two handles under the waveform, or type exact times into the Start and End fields, to select the part you want to keep.

3. Preview it. Click "Preview Selection" to listen to exactly what you're about to cut, before committing to a download.

4. Download. Click "Trim & Download" and a new audio file containing only your selection saves straight to your device as a WAV file.

Why It Matters

Why Trim Audio Locally in Your Browser?

Most free "cut mp3 online" tools upload your file to a server, trim it there, and send it back — which means your audio, even a private voice memo or an unreleased track, passes through someone else's system. This tool decodes and trims the file using your browser's built-in Web Audio API, so the audio data never leaves your device.

It's also faster for most files, since there's no upload or download wait over the network — trimming a five-minute file typically takes under a second once it's loaded.

Good To Know

Tips for Trimming Cleanly

Zoom in mentally on the waveform. Louder sections show as taller peaks, silence shows as a flat line near the center — use this to spot exactly where a word, beat, or pause starts and ends.

Preview before you download. A selection that looks right on the waveform can still cut off the start of a word if you're a fraction of a second early. Always preview first.

For clean cuts, start and end on silence when possible. Trimming mid-word or mid-note is more noticeable than trimming during a natural pause.

The output is a WAV file. WAV is lossless and plays everywhere, but it's larger than MP3. If file size matters for your use case, you can compress the trimmed file afterward with a dedicated audio compressor.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this the same as an MP3 cutter or audio cutter? ▾
Yes — "audio trimmer," "MP3 cutter," and "audio cutter" all describe the same task: selecting and keeping just part of a file. This tool works the same way regardless of what you call it, and handles MP3, WAV, OGG, and M4A input.
Does trimming reduce audio quality?
No. This tool copies the selected samples directly without re-encoding, so the trimmed section keeps the same quality as the original file. The output is exported as WAV, a lossless format.
Is my audio file uploaded to a server?
No. Your audio file is decoded and processed locally in your browser using the Web Audio API. It is never uploaded anywhere.
What audio formats can I trim?
Most browsers can decode MP3, WAV, OGG, and M4A/AAC files. The trimmed output is exported as a WAV file, which is universally supported and lossless.
Is there a file size or length limit?
There's no hard limit set by this tool, but very large files (over roughly 100MB, or very long recordings) may be slow to process depending on your device's memory, since everything runs in your browser rather than on a server.
Why is the downloaded file a WAV instead of MP3?
WAV is a lossless format that any browser can encode without extra software, which keeps this tool simple, fast, and free of quality loss. If you need a smaller MP3 file afterward, you can compress it with a dedicated MP3 converter.
