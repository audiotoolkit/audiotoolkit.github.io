Good To Know

Choosing a Noise Color

White noise has equal energy across all frequencies — the classic "TV static" or fan sound, effective for masking a wide range of background noises.

Pink noise reduces higher frequencies, giving a softer, more natural sound closer to steady rainfall — many people find it easier to relax to than white noise.

Brown noise reduces high frequencies even further for a deep, low rumble similar to a distant waterfall or heavy rain — popular for blocking lower-pitched background sounds like traffic.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between white, pink, and brown noise? ▾
White noise has equal energy at every frequency, giving it a bright, hissy character. Pink noise reduces higher frequencies for a softer, more balanced sound many people find more soothing. Brown noise reduces high frequencies even further, producing a deep, rumbling sound similar to heavy rain or a low fan.
Can I set a timer to stop automatically?
Yes. Choose a duration and the noise will fade out and stop automatically, useful for falling asleep without it playing all night.
Does this download or record anything?
No. The noise is generated live and played directly, with nothing recorded, saved, or downloaded. Simply leave the tab open for as long as you want it playing.
Will this work if I lock my phone?
Behavior varies by browser and device. Some browsers pause audio in background tabs to save battery, so for uninterrupted playback overnight, keep the screen on or check your browser's background audio settings.
