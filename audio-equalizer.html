How It Works

How to Equalize an Audio File

1. Upload your audio. Drag and drop a file into the upload area, or click to browse your device.

2. Adjust the bands, or pick a preset. Move the Bass, Mid, and Treble sliders, or start from a preset like Bass Boost or Vocal Clarity.

3. Preview and download. Listen to the change in real time, then download the processed file as a WAV.

Why It Matters

How the Filtering Works

This equalizer uses the Web Audio API's native filter nodes — a low shelf filter for bass, a peaking filter for mid, and a high shelf filter for treble — the same building blocks used in professional audio software. Processing happens entirely in your browser, so there's no upload wait and no quality loss beyond the intentional tonal adjustment you make.

Good To Know

Tips for Natural-Sounding Adjustments

Small changes go a long way. A boost or cut of 3–6 dB is often enough to notice a clear difference; pushing to the extremes (±12 dB) can sound unnatural or introduce distortion.

Cutting is often more useful than boosting. Reducing a muddy bass or harsh treble range can sound cleaner than boosting other ranges to compensate.

Use "Vocal Clarity" for spoken word. It gently lifts the mid-range where speech intelligibility lives, useful for podcasts or voice recordings that sound muffled.
